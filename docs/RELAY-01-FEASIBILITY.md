This tests an explicitly installed ideal two-stage relay: U converts F to I,
releasing2 units of usable work; V converts I to spent Z, releasing4 more.
Finite F potential6 pays both outputs. Separate components have the same
diffusible intermediate, conversion channels and usable-work access. Joining
changes neither yields nor interactions. This is a necessary-feasibility gate,
not calibrated chemistry, stochastic dynamics or an autonomous controller.

For n consumed resource units, component formation/replacement price c,
per-component upkeep m and association price b, whole-life net work is
`6n-3c-2m` for separate components and `6n-3c-2m-2b` for the joined relay.
Fresh post-damage net work is respectively `6(n-1)-c-2m` and that value minus b.
Thus nonnegative joining prices cannot improve either endpoint. This symbolic
result does not depend on a favourable selected price or yield.

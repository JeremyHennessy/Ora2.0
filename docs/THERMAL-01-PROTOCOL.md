## Distinct mechanism and supplied apparatus

Use two finite thermal resources rather than chemical fuel: a three-level
converter has internal energies (0,3,1). Selective hot contact on 0↔1,
cold contact on 1↔2, and a usable load on 2↔0 can convert three hot units
into two cold units and one work unit. The heat-engine principle is motivated
by [Scovil and Schulz-DuBois](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.2.262).
Our classical integer-reservoir/constructor model is NOT a molecular or quantum
replication. A material body and contact architecture are installed possibilities;
their spontaneous discovery, formation and useful persistence remain untested.

Three labelled raw structural atoms, no initially assembled body. An assembled
body stores3 potential units. Complete construction price c takes EVERY integer
value3..12; none is selected after results. Paying c work stores3 and releases
c-3 to the cold reservoir. At q=0 this reaction is reversible: release bound3
and consume cold c-3 to return c work. There is no free supplied working engine.
The supplied design/constructor is not autonomous origin, even from raw atoms.

Initial hot H=36, cold K=12, usable reserve W=2c+1; total E=49+2c.
The reserve can pay two constructions and destruction; it must be entirely
repaid before any net surplus is accepted. The finite bath multiplicity is
g(H,K)=2^(H+2K), a phenomenological reservoir abstraction. It gives a hot/cold
temperature ratio2, not two infinite heat sources or literal laboratory joules.
Heat, work, bound potential and internal energy must conserve E at every step.
The three material atoms are retained; every rebuild creates a fresh body and
component identities, with prior-body/component ancestry. Retained copies fail.

Hot/cold thermal transitions pay the exact internal energy change from that
reservoir; reverse transitions deposit it. Work2→0 raises W by1; reverse pays1.
For every physical edge use rate p*2^min(0,delta(H+2K)); reverse prefactor p is
identical. Assembly p=2^-c, thermal/work p=1 unless specified below. Refuse
negative reservoirs/work and verify exact integer detailed-balance products.
The common proposal vocabulary is bath x high/low gap x direction, load x
direction, and construction/release. No discarded thermal reverse or hidden drive.

contact strength across both baths on both gaps, with p=1/2 per contact.

and the usable load remain available. Conditional accepted rates can differ.
Costs, atoms, endowments and losses are identical by installed model assumption,
not empirically measured equal hardware costs. Any later physical realization

Shuffled is the prospective uniform mixture of the two contact assignments:
one is the candidate's working assignment, the other hot-low/cold-high.
Enumerate BOTH, never select only the nonworking assignment. Its working
half is an explicit positive null; shuffled mixture can reach the endpoint.
Report the reversed member's failure separately; do not call it a fair primary
comparator by itself. The paired controls are about contact organization, not
an energy-efficiency advantage over the best independent path.

Controlled damage occurs once after FIVE net forward load cycles at q=0.
Pay1 work, release old bound3 to cold, return the atoms to raw state, and then
pay the same complete c construction price. Preserve old/fresh identities.
The operator's destruction is an explicitly priced irreversible intervention,
excluded from physical reversibility. This event-locked oracle does not test
unscreened startup probability or a blind-time natural damage exposure.

## Endpoint, bound and early refusal

At q=0, rebuilt and functioning, require W_final>W_initial AND
W_final>W_immediately_before_damage, with cold K_final>=12 and hot H_final>=0.
The latter comparison includes the paid destruction/rebuild bill, so subsequent
conversion must repay it; do not count reserve-funded replacement as success.
Require new load output after reconstruction through the fresh body's identity.

For the correctly wired engine, every completed net load cycle uses3 hot units.
At q=0 and the registered q=0 loss, H=36-3n, W=n, K=10+2c+2n after two paid
constructions and one loss. Therefore n<=12. Whole-life surplus is n-(2c+1)
and post-damage surplus is n-(c+6). Test all ten costs, all four layouts
(candidate, independent, both shuffled members). No parameter rescue.
Derive and audit all bounds independently; a nonpositive upper bound stops
that cost profile before kinetics. Reversed wiring has H=36+2n and
K=10+2c-3n; with retained cold capital it cannot qualify. An independent path
may use the same hot-high/cold-low cycle as candidate; it must attain the same
endpoint wherever candidate does. Equal best paths exclude a reachability or
maximum-yield advantage, not necessarily a kinetic/renewal advantage.

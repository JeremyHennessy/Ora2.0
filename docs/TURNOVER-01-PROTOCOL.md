## Question and scope

Can paid recovery of atoms from degraded, internally assembled components support
new components that actually use nutrients, under a neutral finite encounter law?
Measure that opportunity separately from effects on total usable work and resource
conversion. The old stationary A/C cycle gave0/16 renewed-chain use and is closed.
This alternative replaces named catalytic roles with a uniform binary-chain law.
It installs physical binding and energy conversion rules, not a reward, searcher,
organism, learning strategy or predetermined production cycle. Any observed use
expresses those installed laws, not a discovered new physical capability.

The old constructor remains a qualitative frozen reference. Different reaction
costs, geometry and species prohibit pooling its counts with this experiment or
attributing cross-model differences to turnover alone. Within the new law, local
stock/paid-ghost controls isolate paid recovery; a shared-stock spatial shortcut
also challenges whether extra local organization is useful.

## Uniform law and complete ledgers

Every atom has immutable ID and bit0/1, external genesis origin (monomer/nutrient),
and recorded degradation/reclaim history. Objects contain ordered atom IDs, a
binary sequence, site, birth tick and consumed parent IDs. Initial objects are
only single monomers; no functional polymer, producer or selected founder is
supplied. Object IDs are never reused. This is compositional ancestry, not birth
of new atoms or organism reproduction.

Ligation of two different local objects x,y (including monomers) concatenates
their sequences/atom lists when total length<=6. Cost2W: one unit becomes the new
bond's stored potential, one heat. No catalyst or successful-outcome classifier
chooses the product. Each chain stores length-1 bond potential. Decay of any
selected object transfers all its atoms to local waste and releases all bond
potential as heat. Waste cannot ligate. Recover one local waste atom at cost1W
to heat1; it becomes a new monomer with its same atom identity. No free recycling.

Contact pays read1W then, if possible, process1W. A polymer length>=2 whose last
bit complements the local nutrient bit can convert one nutrient atom (mass1,
potential8) to local waste, returning3W and releasing5heat. Total contact delta
including costs is +1W/+7heat. A monomer cannot bind. Missing/nonbinding targets
still pay the funded read/process charges; W1 pays only read and cannot convert.
Nutrient atoms remain accounted for and can later be recovered at normal cost.
Passive motion moves one object to a neighboring ring site without work/heat;
neutral damage/motion and global W are explicit environmental scaffolds.

At every event: each atom appears exactly once in an object, waste or unused
nutrient; sequence bits agree with atom identities; per-bit material is conserved;
W + heat + sum(chain length-1) +8*remaining nutrients equals initial energy.
No atom duplication, external replenishment, uncharged recovery or bond energy
creation. Passive upkeep0 is a favorable modeling assumption, not maintenance.

Object/waste/nutrient candidates are sorted by string ID; selector modulo pool
size picks a target, so no observer evaluates desirability. For ligation pick
left, then right from remaining objects. Proposed ID b{tick}; reclaim ID r{tick}.

Actions0/1 ligate;2 recover;3 contact;4 decay;5 move (left selector picks object,

no additional birth/damage, delayed retries or extra horizon. Retain all128 events
even after work exhaustion. Failed proposals create no object and no cost except
the explicitly funded contact/read or paid-ghost recovery. Every record preserves
genesis, complete draws/events, before/after hashes, costs/outcomes and terminal
state; regenerating the full request stream prevents selected-history reporting.

## Seven paired arms

| Arm | Intervention |
|---|---|
| active | Full local law, paid recovery and binding-dependent capture |
| fixed | Independent fixed evaluator of the same binding relation; exact active match required |
| ghost_reclaim | If local waste/work allow recovery, pay1W/heat1 but leave atom in waste |
| ghost_capture | Real paid recovery/assembly; funded contacts pay normally but cannot convert nutrients |
| local_stock | Local grammar, no recovery and no recovery charge; finite original feedstock only |
| shared_stock | No recovery/charge; ligation may draw any global objects into requested site at normal price |
| direct_access | Full paid local recovery/assembly; funded nutrient contact converts without binding, explicitly engine-supplied access |

Initial material/energy and proposals match. Active/ghost recovery price matches
per eligible attempt; later pools, spending and trajectories may diverge. Shared
stock supplies free global precursor access and is a labeled spatial shortcut,
not a trajectory-matched control. Direct access is an installed bypass, not a
new producer. Report its outcomes separately. Fixed match cannot refute emergence
in general; here there is no independent adaptive mechanism to distinguish.

## Endpoints, verification, decision

Primary opportunity: a nutrient conversion by an internally ligated polymer
containing an atom previously recovered **after decay of an internally ligated
polymer**. The later polymer must be born after that reclaim. Nutrient-waste
assimilation alone, recovered genesis monomers, repeated use of an unchanged
component, ghost effects and direct-access conversions do not qualify. Count
distinct qualifying polymers and uses separately; record full external atom roots.

polymer-origin recoveries versus simple/nutrient waste, total conversions,

## Information supplied by an actual attempt

Let P be the chosen port bit, T the target of a real local packet, and F the
truthful outcome. The law is F = 1 exactly when P = T. Therefore a real
attempt identifies its target as T = P XOR (1 XOR F), if its chosen port and
the validity of the attempt are known. This is a truth-table identity, not
an authored VM strategy, installed updating solution or learning result.

On failure the same packet remains. A remaining warm packet changes target
at tick 64, so its identity alone does not imply a constant target across
that boundary. A fresh packet's target is fixed after release. On success
the packet is removed and
the next sorted available packet can have a different target. Consequently,
information about the attempted target is not automatically a correct
prediction of the next target. Repeated F reads before another ACT copy the
same observation; they are not fresh trials or independent samples.

Initial F and F after an empty ACT are zero. An organism has no explicit
valid-attempt flag or packet identity in this source language. Thus F = 0
alone cannot distinguish an empty/reset condition from a real failed attempt.
Tracking chosen ports, changes, availability or prior actions must use the
organism's actual reachable state and paid instructions, not auditor labels.
Controls31's own-disrupted read may numerically match truthful F; that does
not turn its unrelated supplied bit into personal history.

## Representational ceiling, not reachability proof

For each fixed thirteen-triple genotype, the (r, m, PC, F) projection has at
most 16^10 * 13 * 2 states. Registers and memory account for forty bits;
PC and F add a factor twenty-six. This is an upper bound, not a measured
reachable-state count, independent bit count or useful-history capacity.
Many states may be inaccessible from cold zero initialization.

CONST, XOR, memory and zero-test branches can implement more than an affine
one-step response. Branches depend on full four-bit zero, while ACT depends
only on a register's low bit. Do not reduce these programs to a truth table
of latest F/P alone or declare counting impossible from the absence of ADD.
There is currently no paid construction or general representability proof
for a longer-history estimator. Supplying one as a successful candidate
would conflict with the approved performance-blind sourcing boundary.

## Necessary paid bounds under unchanged prices

Founder formation is 3N + 16. N = 13 costs 55, leaves 457 free material
units and 73 constructor energy; startup energy is 12. Release at tick 64
uses 16 constructor energy and does not replenish the organism. Each
instruction costs one organism energy, including NOP and failed ACT.
Success can add at most twenty, capped at sixty-four; excess becomes heat.

In this immutable one-founder local scope, endpoint completion needs all
sixteen warm and sixteen fresh packets. One instruction can collect at most
one packet; fresh packets are unavailable before tick 64. Even if every
fresh ACT succeeds, sixteen fresh successes require completion no earlier
than tick 79. Thus the thirteen-triple completion bill is at least 55 + 79
= 134. This is a necessary bound only: no reachable trajectory or viable
processor has been certified. Future broader/multientity scopes need their
own bounds rather than silently reusing this one.

If energy immediately after a success is e, a next success d instructions
later can rescue an ACT paid down to zero when d = e. If d > e, an earlier
non-success step already kills this single organism. Conditional on an
otherwise lawful surviving interval, the next energy is min(64, e-d+20).
Consecutive misses, control flow, empty attempts and feedback processing all
consume that same interval budget. Average intake greater than average cost
does not certify transient survival. COPY/DIV and daughter viability remain
outside this learning-only source scope; these bounds are not reproduction
cost estimates and do not alter SINGLECOPY09.

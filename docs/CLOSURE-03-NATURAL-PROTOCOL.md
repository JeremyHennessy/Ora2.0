## Question

Does a damaged population naturally produce its removed prerequisite within
the frozen finite budget? When the first such event has an available matched
token, does removal of the newborn change later paid causal payload restoration
relative to equal-material removal of that other token? No encounter is
scheduled to produce repair. The conditional comparison applies only to
exposed, matchable populations, not all random worlds.

## Natural trigger and structural match

Monitor post-damage collisions without altering them. Count all attempted
P-producing collisions, including resource-starved attempts, and actual P
births. The trigger is the first actual P birth after damage in a motif-present
population, regardless of damage eligibility. Never wait for a later birth to
obtain a better match or outcome. If no trigger occurs, report unexposed.

Immediately before that birth, enumerate live tokens excluding the two parent
IDs. A matched token must have a payload different from P and T and share P's
first and last coordinates: equal potential incoming/outgoing gate degree and
four material units. Choose lexicographically first ID. If absent, record
exposed-unmatched and continue baseline only. Do not assume that this other
token is functionally irrelevant: age, ancestry and productive roles differ.
It is a matched-other-token control, not proof of a biologically inert sham.

The newborn's birth is paid identically before intervention. If a match exists,
use three external laboratory counterfactual continuations of the same prefix:
intact, remove-newborn-P, remove-matched-other. Explicitly tagged intervention
decay converts exactly four material to waste in each removal arm; no added
fuel or unlogged material is charged by decay under this installed law.
No probe births or manual encounters, no externally rescued components.
Baseline keeps its birth and therefore is not material-matched to removal.
Prefix replay is an implementation of experimental state copying; it is not
population reproduction, independent extra seeds or organismal offspring.

Apply removal immediately after the trigger collision and before that tick's
background decay. Continue remaining ticks using the same u/v/w stream and
ordinary branch-specific live-ID selection. No repeated intervention. Different
populations can consume different total material/fuel; report totals and do
not claim trajectory cost matching. Removing newborn P is not removal of a
closed producer network, so even a positive effect supports only this local
causal question. Fixed-table equivalence of the splice law remains a limitation.

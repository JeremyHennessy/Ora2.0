# Founding Research Charter

**Status:** design hypotheses only, 2026-10-08. No empirical result or artificial organism is claimed.

## Question

Can a computational substrate yield persistent self-maintaining organization, adaptive behaviors and heritable novelty that continue to develop without us specifying the ultimate creature, intelligence, language, or social role?

**Do not conflate this with creating consciousness.** Whether a digital process can be alive, conscious, or have subjective experience is unsettled and requires distinct philosophical and scientific treatment. This program studies observable properties.

## Strict independence from previous Ora projects

**User direction (2026-10-08): Ora2.0 must not be influenced by the original Ora/AgentTest project or its problems.**

- The shared **name** does not imply architectural, experimental, developmental, or historical continuity.
- **Do not import, copy, adapt, or use** legacy code, phases, benchmarks, rewards, goals, prompts, UI, observer architecture, handoffs, experiments, roadmaps, or data as a starting point.
- **Do not design around** old Ora's bugs, failures, operational incidents, constraints, or proposed fixes. No “lessons learned from previous Ora” as requirements or design guidance.
- No inherited stage numbers, aspirations, intelligence milestones, agent personalities, or continuation of a prior organism.
- All future design decisions must be independently justified by **artificial-life science, explicit new hypotheses, and experiments performed for Ora2.0 itself**.
- Standard software safety, verification, provenance, and reproducibility practices are allowed, but must stand on their own merits rather than being imported as legacy-project solutions.
- Only revisit legacy Ora material if the user specifically requests a comparison or authorizes an exception; otherwise keep it out of design reviews and research inputs.

## What “no goals” does and does not mean

- Do not impose chatbot utility, human imitation, predefined skill curricula, or a targeted intelligence score.
- Do not write a hidden “become alive” reward and then call maximizing it emergent life.
- Even the simplest world has *designer-chosen* state variables, physics, resources, termination conditions, and observation windows. Natural selection can emerge from those rules. Document them honestly.
- Compare systems with explicit rewards, systems with only viability constraints, and systems with no organism-level reward signal. If an objective is necessary, record it rather than disguising it.

## Candidate substrates — none approved as the winner

### A. Artificial chemistry / protocell organization

Local interaction rules, energy/material accounting, reaction networks, membrane or boundary formation, persistence under perturbation. Core risk: authored reaction rules can predetermine every apparent innovation.

### B. Digital evolution / executable heredity

Replicating programs or structured automata with mutations, resource limits, lineage tracking and ecological interactions. Core risk: brittle replication, trivial selection, or early evolutionary stagnation.

### C. Developmental / cellular systems

Local state updates, morphogenesis, repair and adaptive sensorimotor couplings (including non-neural alternatives). Core risk: interesting patterns generated by externally trained parameters without durable self-maintenance or open-ended novelty.

### D. Other evidence-backed candidates

Add only with sources and a falsifiable comparison. Do not merge architectures merely because each is fashionable.

## Measurable questions

1. **Persistence:** Does a coherent individual remain identifiable through state changes, rather than merely preserving a static file?
2. **Maintenance:** Does it actively restore relevant organization after controlled disturbances? Compared to passive or scripted controls?
3. **Resource accounting:** Are resource flows, costs, and replenishment explicit and conserved or bounded as the model requires?
4. **Heredity:** Is variation transmitted over generations and causally linked to descendant properties?
5. **Within-lifetime adaptation:** Does experience improve response to unfamiliar changes compared with a frozen or randomized control?
6. **Ecological interaction:** Do population relationships measurably affect survival, organization, or variation?
7. **Novelty:** Do independently specified functional tests detect new mechanisms and not merely new visual patterns?
8. **Open-endedness:** Does the *rate and breadth* of functional innovation persist over long horizons and new environments, instead of saturating a fixed task list?

No single metric establishes life. Selection bias, observer-chosen thresholds, and adversarial examples must be studied.

## Experimental rules

- Preregister hypotheses and controls before looking at held-out outcomes.
- Always include null/random, hard-coded, and simpler mechanistic controls where appropriate.
- State random seeds, substrate/environment versions, compute budget, simulation time, and uncertainty estimates.
- Repeat experiments across seeds; retain *negative* and null results.
- Separate observed events, derived interpretations, predictions, and independent checks.
- A checkpoint, animation, or benchmark increase is not proof of the underlying scientific claim.
- Do not use live internet, developer credentials, or arbitrary host code execution as an organism's environment by default.
- Treat all self-modification as sandboxed experimental data unless explicit new approval changes that boundary.

## Evidence labels

- **E0:** speculative mechanism or question.
- **E1:** primary source located and its claim summarized.
- **E2:** full methods reviewed and implementation assumptions extracted.
- **E3:** local reproducible result with recorded artifact and controls.
- **E4:** independent replication under materially different conditions.

Use the canonical evidence standards in AI-Research where they differ; these labels are a local working summary, not a claim of research-wide consensus.

## Preservation

Record each reviewed checkpoint as a revision + configuration + seed + environment + code/data hashes + run logs + observer snapshot when applicable. Approved baselines are immutable references. Any experiment branches from a known checkpoint and must permit exact rollback.

## Deliberately unresolved

Computational substrate, organism encoding, exact conditions for individuality, resource chemistry, selection mechanism, genome, learning architecture, simulation dimensionality, runtime service, dashboard visual design, and required hardware. Evidence and controlled experiments should decide them.

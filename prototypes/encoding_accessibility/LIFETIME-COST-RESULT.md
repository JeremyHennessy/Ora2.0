## What the copying bill includes

For six code bits the bill is six read/CPU units plus six DNA-formation units,
one blank-state formation unit, one division CPU unit and an eight-unit startup
transfer: **14 heat + 8 transferred = 22**. The daughter starts with the copied
bits and blank private state. Startup is transferred, never created or charged
twice. Known operation heat, copying heat and transferred energy are conserved.

The founder's already formed seven-unit body and initial eight-unit energy grant
are externally supplied setup. The copy fixture also supplies seven free material
units; this work does not prove that the parent acquired those materials. Charging
an already formed daughter its formation cost again would be double charging.

## Full cost closure has not passed

The following remain explicitly unknown, never silently priced at zero:

* Program-decoder/control energy per dispatch and physical storage maintenance.
* Acquisition and custody of the copying material stock.
* Copying clock, resource-phase changes, expiry and lawful child release.
* The mapping between host/fork resource consumption and organism energy.

The old toy storage-zero quote is preserved; it is not proof that physical
storage is free. Host CPU, RAM, wall time and storage are separate physical
quantities unless an independently justified price mapping is registered.
The verifier returns full-cost qualification false in every case. It supplies
no native Ora, population or launch authority.

There is a useful necessary bound before the first reward. With delay `d`, the
known attempt/feedback/delay/first-probe bill is `4+d`, across `3+d` dispatches.
With eight starting units, all additional pre-reward costs must fit `4-d` units.
If an extra decoder fee is uniform in the same toy energy units, it must be no
more than `(4-d)/(3+d)`: **2/5 at delay two, 1/6 at delay three**. At equality a
successful probe can rescue the actor with its atomic reward; a failed probe
leaves zero and stops it. Survival after a failed probe needs a strict margin.
These are optimistic affordability bounds, not measured decoder prices or
sufficient lifetime/reproductive conditions. They do not justify changing prices.
